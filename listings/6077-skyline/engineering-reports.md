## 2. Act 1 — Bear (pre-flip, pre-"cure"): the disease, documented

**The chassis:** 1961 one-story wood-frame on **drilled piers + reinforced concrete grade
beams** with interior drilled-pier footings and posts — a foundation type with typically
deep embedment. Crawlspace dry at inspection *except* the right-front and front perimeter.

### 2.1 Foundation cracks
- Approximately **9 cracks, ¼″ or less**, in the crawlspace perimeter grade beam.
- Stated causes: *"shallow foundation embedment depth and **expansive soils** which will
  swell when wet and contract when dry, **foundation settlement**, or **past seismic
  activity**."* — a moving house on reactive soil, not cosmetic shrinkage.
- Recommended fix: **Option 3 — pressure-injected structural epoxy** (~$4.5K ±30% total;
  ~$500/crack).
- **Separately:** cracks in concrete that was **added to the existing foundation** at the
  middle of the rear side — a prior addition's foundation already cracking; patch with
  repair mortar.

### 2.2 Drainage — the real villain (five separate findings)
1. **Exterior grade slopes TOWARD the foundation** on the right side — the same side
   where moisture intrusion was observed. Options: regrade; sloped hardscape (~$6K ±30%
   per side); full French drains (~$45K ±30% reference). Recommendation: regrade +
   hardscape combination.
2. **No positive drainage under the deck** — *"Water control around properties is
   critical to the longevity of the foundation."* → subsurface drainage (French drain or
   similar) along the front side.
3. **Downspouts/gutters** discharging at the foundation — discharge must be ≥5 ft away;
   add elbows at two downspouts; properly connect another.
4. **Crawlspace slightly damp** at right-front/front perimeter — "most likely from the
   surface drainage conditions." Monitor during winter/after heavy rain.
5. **Subsurface systems** need periodic inspection/cleaning to ensure flow away from the
   foundation.

### 2.3 The failed retaining wall
- *"The retaining wall shown in Image 1 **has failed** and replacement is necessary"* —
  ~**26 ft** long.
- Cost: *"likely in excess of **$13K**"* — and replacement *"would first require
  **engineering** (and potentially a **soils report**)."*
- Two additional walls **leaning a few degrees** (monitor); cracked sections on others to
  be **rebar-stitched, epoxied, sealed** (> $1K).

### 2.4 Water damage and rot
- Rot/water damage at the **middle of the front crawlspace**: *"Water damage and rot can
  lead to **loss of structural integrity**."*
- Clean/treat, replace significantly damaged members in kind, **get a pest/WDO
  inspection** (the later HomeGuard termite report obliged: fungus at 7 Section-1
  locations + **damp-wood termites**).

### 2.5 Secondary items
- **Chemical spalling** (left-front crawlspace): surface-level on probing — *"Had the
  foundation crumbled in areas when probed it would be of greater concern, but that was
  not the case."* Monitor in 3–5 years; improve water flow.
- **Efflorescence**: indicator of excess moisture migrating through the foundation.
- **Wood-to-soil contact** mid-crawlspace: lower soil to ≥6″ clearance; inspect for
  rot/termites.
- **Exposed rebar** (front corners) and **minor slab cracks**: minor-cost items.

### 2.6 Seismic
- Property is in a high-seismic region **and within a CGS landslide hazard zone** (Bear
  says so explicitly). Existing detailing *"consistent with the age of construction"* —
  i.e., **no retrofit exists**. ATC-50-1 scope recommended: mud-sill anchors, rim-joist
  and post connectors, cripple-wall plywood bracing.

### 2.7 Bear's conclusion — the cushion, with conditions
> *"…the structure's visible foundation systems are in **serviceable condition** when
> compared to similar homes of this age, design and site conditions. The observed systems
> appear to remain capable of performing their intended purpose **with the completion of
> maintenance and repair items noted in this report**. We did not observe systems or
> conditions that presented an **imminent threat**…"*

Scope limits that matter: visual only; **no soils investigation, no slope-stability
analysis, no drainage analysis** — on a state-mapped Landslide Zone of Required
Investigation. Their buyer summary (doc 39) put the clearly-quantified minimum at
**$18.5K+** before drainage design, engineering/soils, wood-rot, or seismic work.

**Pre-cure bottom line:** a repairable house whose foundation problem is *driven by a
water problem*, with a failed wall that must be *engineered* back, and an explicit
geotech gap.

## 3. Interlude — the "cure": 15/15, self-certified (doc 41)

The completion report claims all 15 Bear items were addressed **May–July 2026**. The
paper is the problem:

1. **No contractor named. No permits cited. No engineer sign-off. No invoices.** For
   structural work on a landslide-zone lot — exactly the paper AB 968 exists to require.
2. **The two biggest invisible scopes have zero completion photographs:** the French
   drain (item 3) and the **seismic retrofit** (item 9 — anchors, connectors,
   cripple-wall plywood). Own note: *"Items without completion photographs were completed
   as described; photographs were not taken for those items."*
3. The failed 26-ft wall — which Bear said needed **engineering and possibly a soils
   report first** — was *"replaced with a **new wood retaining wall**."* No engineering,
   soils report, or drainage design mentioned anywhere.
4. **Timing:** work finished ~July, but completion photos and the report are dated
   **2026-10-04 — six days after the escrow fell**. A document manufactured for the
   re-list.

## 4. Act 2 — Padir (post-"cure", 2026-10-05): condemned again

Seven days after the escrow fell, the agent and seller bought a second opinion. Padir
does credit part of the cure — then re-condemns the house.

### 4.0 What Padir credits (for fairness)
No exterior-wall out-of-plumb; no significant open structural cracks; repaired areas show
no new cracking ("sign of wall repair was observed… no crack was observed"); no severe
rot; wood moisture at natural levels; guardrails essentially code-compliant (one >4″
under-rail gap at the main stairs flagged for future upgrade); small exterior cracks
(<⅛″) need no repair *if the cause is removed*; "the building is safe" for those cracks.
(Padir also describes the property sloppily as "two-story… with an attached garage" and
worked partly from supplied photographs — note it, but the findings below are
measurement-based.)

### 4.1 NEW defect Bear never flagged: the load-bearing crawlspace retaining wall
- ~**12.5 ft** of the wall's top layer rotated **5.4°** — **1″–3.3″ out of plumb** vs an
  ACI 117-10 construction tolerance of **0.108″** for a 3-ft wall.
- Cause: *"water leakage in the rear yard and dripping behind this wall, and also a
  **damaged drainage pipe**… the soil moisture confirmed it."*
- Inference: *"there should be a **severe crack behind the wall** or a **complete
  foundation rotation**."*
- Criticality: *"Since this wall **carries the load of the upper-level post**, repair and
  reinforcement of this wall **is required**."*
- Remedy: ~20 ft of new drainage behind the wall; repair the yard pipes; **concrete
  buttress / steel supports (~5 @ 3.5′ spacing)** — ***"before the rainy season."***
- Chapter 9 links it to the flip itself: ***"The new addition shows out-of-plumb
  conditions"*** investigated in that chapter.

### 4.2 The cure itself is condemned
> *"The retaining wall is new, and there is **no drainage material behind the wall**.
> Therefore, the current condition will increase the moisture content behind the wall and
> may saturate the soil next to the building foundation and the retaining wall.
> Consequently, **this new setup will cause cracks in the building within a year, and the
> retaining wall will show lateral deformation**."*

They replaced a failed wall with a cheaper wood wall its own engineer predicts will fail
— because nobody fixed the water.

### 4.3 Settlement is still active
- *"The foundation shows **minor settlement**"* (§8.2.1).
- Corner vertical crack *"resulted from **corner settlement**… due to a **water drainage
  problem** at this corner"* — specifically *"the puncture and leak of water from the
  following drainage system."*
- **Efflorescence** — Bear's moisture marker — still present *"in multiple locations"*
  **after** the claimed drainage cure.

### 4.4 Water is still getting in
- Crawlspace (§13.2): *"We observed **signs of water intrusion** in some areas."*
- Downspouts (§10.1): two of them *"are the source of the water leak to the lower level
  and behind the retaining wall."*
- §10.2: multiple downspouts still discharge within 5 ft of the foundation; one discharge
  pipe must be replaced and extended **5 ft beyond the rear retaining wall**; a
  crawlspace pipe still leaks ("Repair this pipe in the crawlspace and ensure there is no
  leak").

### 4.5 New punch items
- **Water-heater straps noncompliant** — replace at ⅓ tank height (**"Immediate
  actions"** item). This is the flip's brand-new water heater — and the WHSD/TDS §D
  certification is currently false on this point.
- **Front walkway offset ≥¾″** — *"grind this step to prevent a trip hazard and
  **potential lawsuit**."*

### 4.6 The structural tell
Padir's action plan contains **"Plan for repairs (in one year)"**: maintain positive
drainage, modify the front sidewalk, *"review the requirements for the footing,"* repair
the interior retaining wall. **A cured house does not come with a one-year repair plan.**

## 6. The arc, and what it buys you

**Bear** diagnosed a water-driven foundation problem and issued a *conditional*
serviceable verdict. The seller **papered over it** with an unpermitted, self-certified,
largely photo-less "cure" — in at least one case (the wood retaining wall) structurally
self-defeating. **Padir — their own engineer, hired to reassure buyers after the escrow
fell** — documents that the water is still winning, a load-bearing wall is rotating
toward failure, and the house is on a repair clock that runs out when the rains start.

Consequences:

1. **Disclosure exposure (their side):** SPQ 8.A, TDS §II.B, TDS C.7/C.8 — all answered
   "No" on 2026-08-07/11, *after* the May–July work and the July inspections. False
   against their own two-engineer record. See `disclosure-analysis.md` §2 (items 2–5).
2. **Leverage (your side):** the repair scope is already engineered and sequenced *by the
   seller's own consultant* — buttress (~5 supports @3.5′) + ~20 ft rear drainage + pipe
   repairs + WH-strap correction + walkway grind — plus the roof report's replacement
   scope. Two independent contractor bids per scope convert directly into the
   credit/holdback math in `STRATEGY.md` §8.
3. **Timing (your side):** "before the rainy season" is the seller's deadline pressure,
   not yours. Every week of delay costs them carrying margin on a cash purchase and moves
   the un-cured wall closer to its first winter.
4. **Gate status (`STRATEGY.md` §9):** Gate 3 (structure) fails today on Padir alone;
   Gate 1 (permits/AB 968) fails as presented; the "cure narrative" is not credible — all
   cure claims must be re-verified independently (contractor identity, CSLB, permits,
   photos, WDO clearance).
