## 3. Areas of concern (severity-ordered)

1. **Water intrusion complex (high).** Lower level flooded twice in El Niño years
   (SE corner + east wall); **garage foundation is designed to let water pass
   through** — garage floor/walls "often wet during rains" (seller's own words);
   hall-bath floor dry rot from a shower leak (rebuilt for sale); kitchen-sink
   cabinet rot found during sale prep; ice-maker line leaked twice; 2026 Duval
   french drain (south + internal east) **untested by heavy rain**; BPG adds
   **negative grading at right rear** + downspouts discharging at the building +
   gutter overflow "fixed" 10/28 but never rain-tested. Winter 2026-27 will be the
   first real test. This is the house's defining defect pattern.
2. **Hillside stability (high).** State **Landslide Seismic Hazard Zone** (NHD).
   Seller-disclosed soil erosion that **worsened after eucalyptus removal, deck and
   drain work**; 2022 Mariposa hillside-stabilization bids ($5.8K / $15.3K)
   **never done**; DIY retaining walls "leaning" with "rot in the posts", "some posts
   will need replacing sooner than later"; BPG: structural retaining walls not
   analyzed, and the **upper lot (walls/decks above the studio) was excluded from
   inspection**. Geotech + structural engineer are mandatory diligence here.
3. **Seismic readiness (medium-high).** BPG: anchor bolts don't meet current
   standards; pier-beam connections in garage "only marginally secure" (recommends
   T-straps); garage-door corner bracing unknown (finished walls); CMU block
   foundation portion "may not be as structurally sound as concrete." Hillside
   home in a landslide zone → get a retrofit bid.
4. **Fire/insurance (high, but statutory position improved — verified).** The NHD
   (9/8/26) says **not** in a High/Very High FHSZ. I verified against CAL FIRE's
   2025 LRA map (FHSALRA25_v1_All): the parcel coords return **"NonWildland"**,
   with Very High zones beginning ~0.3 mi east/southeast. So the statutory FHSZ
   disclosure is clean and AB38 FHDS likely not required. **But:** the parcel sits
   in the 1991 firestorm footprint; seller describes **annual Oakland Fire Dept
   vegetation inspections** (SPQ 17.G); the 2025-26 tax bill includes **"WILDFIRE
   PREV - MM" $99** (levied only inside Oakland's wildfire prevention area);
   combustible cedar-shingle siding + wood decks + ivy on walls. Insurers use
   proprietary risk models, not CAL FIRE maps — **quote insurance BEFORE offering**;
   availability in 94611 is not assured.
5. **Unpermitted studio/treehouse (medium).** Built 2015 by owners+friends — "no
   plans, permits, engineers"; post/stilt-founded on the slope; **electricity run to
   it in 2025** (also unpermitted, apparently); excluded from BPG's scope; excluded

## 5. Offer-relevant leverage

**Seller's financial position (the core of the negotiation):**
- Paid $910K (1/2021), financed $835K via seller carryback, still of record.
- Listed $895K → net before costs ≈ $60K minus commissions/taxes/fees ≈ near zero.
  **Their floor is payoff + costs; their hope is a bid-up.** A 5-year balloon (if any)
  would have matured ~Jan 2026 — if so, they are under real deadline pressure.
- As their own listing agent they save ~2.5% — more room than a normal seller at
  the same price, but the carryback math dominates.
- Vacant + already purchased in Elmwood = carrying costs + certainty preference.

**Market tension:** hills listings are going +26% to +86% over ask; DOM 1. If this
gets an offer date and multiple bids, inspection leverage evaporates — decide in
advance which risks you'd price (hillside, water, unpermitted studio) rather than
investigate. If it sits past 2-3 weekends, the leverage flips hard: the missing
pest report, the CE case, the unpermitted 2026 work, and the flooding history are
all legitimate credit/price levers, and the seller-agent knows exactly how they read.

**Non-price asks that cost the seller little:**
- Deliver the East Bay Structural pest report (they commissioned it) + CE2000130
  closure documentation + RE2402807 final + deck special-inspection letters.
- Complete the primary-bath light fix (contractor already committed) + HVAC service.
- Transfer the Alley Cat warranty in writing; deliver roof warranty + all
  contractor invoices (Blanco, Duval full billing, Advanced Electrical).
- Include Jarvis plans (deck + kitchen enlargement option) — real future value.
- Written benefit/payoff statement for the Southworth note early in escrow.
- Encroachment: at minimum, an ALTA/Homeowner's policy with survey coverage;
  ideally signed encroachment agreements with 300 Capricorn (and the south
  neighbors) before close — seller has good neighbor relations to leverage.
- Insurance: binding quote (or seller's current carrier assumption) as a
  contingency condition — do this first, before spending on inspections.

**Buyer diligence plan (in priority order):**
1. Insurance quote (availability + premium) — gate item.
2. Independent general inspection + **structural pest inspection**.
3. Geotechnical/soils + structural evaluation (hillside, retaining walls, studio
   posts, garage pier-beam straps, seismic retrofit scope).
4. Sewer lateral video scan (full length, not just the 2021-tested segment).
5. Level II chimney/fireplace inspection.
6. Roof walk (BPG used binoculars from the ground) + gutter performance.
7. Drainage/foundation specialist (garage water-by-design + french-drain as-built).
8. Window inventory (single vs double pane vs plexiglass) during showings.
9. Verify permit file at Oakland Accela (9 records; CE2000130 file pull).
10. Title: order updated prelim (current one is 7/10/2026) + survey/encroachment
    coverage discussion.

## 6. Bottom line

The package is unusually honest in prose (the seller-agent disclosed flooding,
erosion, encroachments, rats, noise, and theft — more than most), but it is
**document-thin where it counts**: the promised pest report is absent, a code-
enforcement abatement is contradicted by a "No" on the TDS, most 2024-2026 work
is unpermitted, and the engineering story of the hillside (stabilization bids,
drainage design, retaining-wall condition) is proposals-and-invoices rather than
completed work. The two structural facts that frame everything: **a house with a
designed-to-leak garage on a landslide-zoned, eroding slope**, and **a seller whose
$835K carryback leaves ~zero equity at list price**. The 2025 FHSZ remap to
"NonWildland" is genuinely good news on paper — but insurance reality, not the
state map, will price the fire risk. Quote insurance first; then let hillside +
water diligence decide whether $895K (or more) is defensible.
